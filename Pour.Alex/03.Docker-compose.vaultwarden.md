# Project 3: Vaultwarden (Your Private Vault)

Now that you have the Docker engine running, let's look at the ultimate "sovereign data" app. **Vaultwarden** is a private, local version of Bitwarden. It keeps your passwords, notes, and 2FA codes on **your** hard drive - not on a tech company's cloud.

### The "No Door" Security Policy
Before we start, it is important to understand why this is more secure than a standard website:
*  **The Recipe is Local:** Even though you need the internet to download the "ingredients" (the Docker image), once it is cooking on your PC, it stays there.
*  **No Door Available:** Because this server is running inside your home network and we haven't told your router to let anyone in, there is **no door** for a hacker on the internet to even find.
*  **Strictly Offline:** Day-to-day use (searching passwords or getting login codes) happens entirely between your devices and this folder.
*  **HTTPS and SSL:** We will add a local HTTPS to your vault. Modern browsers require this "secure" connection to let you use encryption features. We will use a second tool called Caddy to handle this entirely inside your house.
*  **Local-Only Security:**  This setup creates its own security certificate. Even though your browser might say "Not Private," the data is encrypted locally and never leaves your home network. There is still "no door" for anyone on the internet to enter.

### The Vaultwarden "Recipe"

#### Step 1: Prep the Kitchen
1.  Open **PowerShell**.
2.  Type `ipconfig` and hit Enter.
3.  Look for IPv4 Address. It usually looks like `192.168.x.x` Keep this number handy.
4.  Create a dedicated folder for this project: `C:\Docker\vaultwarden`.

#### Step 2: Gather the Ingredients
Go to the folder: `cd C:\Docker\vaultwarden` (You need two create and save the two files there)

##### **File 1:** `docker-compose.yml`

```yaml
services:
  vaultwarden:
    image: vaultwarden/server:latest
    container_name: vaultwarden
    restart: always
    environment:
      - SIGNUPS_ALLOWED=true # Set to 'false' after you create your account
    volumes:
      - ./vw-data:/data

  caddy:
    image: caddy:2
    container_name: caddy
    restart: always
    ports:
      - 11001:80
      - 11002:443
    volumes:
      - ./Caddyfile:/etc/caddy/Caddyfile:ro
      - ./caddy-data:/data
      - ./caddy-config:/config
```
##### **File 2:** `Caddyfile` (Save as `Caddyfile` with no extension. Replace `YOUR_IP` with the number from Step 1)

``` text
{
    default_sni YOUR_IP
}

https://YOUR_IP:443 {
    tls internal
    reverse_proxy vaultwarden:80
}
```

#### Step 3: Cook the Project
1.  Open **PowerShell**.
2.  Go to the folder: `cd C:\Docker\vaultwarden`
3.  Pull the ingredients: `docker compose pull`
4.  Start the cooking: `docker compose up -d`
5.  Open your browser to: `https://YOUR_IP:11002`
6.  Click `Advanced` > `Proceed` to bypass the local certificate warning.

#### Step 4: Android Setup
1. Open Bitwarden app on Android when connected to your local wifi.
2. Tap the **Gear icon** on the login screen.
3. Enter `https://YOUR_IP:11002` in the **Server URL** field.
4. Save and Log In!

## How to Use It
*  **Access:** Open your browser to `https://YOUR_IP:11002` and click `Advanced` > `Proceed` to bypass the local certificate warning the first time you connect with a browser.
*  **Account:** Create your first (and only) account. This is **local to your machine**. 
*  **Locking the Door:** Once you have made your account, edit your recipe file.
 Change `SIGNUPS _ALLOWED=true` to `SIGNUPS _ALLOWED=false`, and run `docker compose up -d` again to restart it.
 This "updates" the kitchen and ensures that no one else on your Wi-Fi can create an account.
